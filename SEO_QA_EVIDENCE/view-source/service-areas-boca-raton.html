Florida's 14-day PIP window

Florida PIP generally requires initial services and care within 14 days of a motor vehicle accident. Eligibility, reimbursement, and benefit limits depend on the policy and circumstances. Waiting to see whether pain resolves on its own can leave you without timely documentation for your claim.

Emergency Medical Condition status and your coverage limit

Florida law also distinguishes PIP claims based on whether a qualifying medical provider diagnoses an Emergency Medical Condition (EMC), which affects the benefit limit available under a policy. Ask your insurer, your other treating providers, or a qualified legal professional how this applies to your specific claim — this page is general information, not legal advice.

What a home-visit evaluation includes

A home visit typically starts the same way an in-office evaluation would: a review of how the accident happened, a hands-on exam of the affected area, and a plan for next steps. The setting changes — the clinical process doesn't.

A mobile visit compared to driving in for care

After a car accident, sitting upright in a vehicle for a clinic visit can aggravate a stiff or strained neck and back before it's even been evaluated. A home visit lets eligible Boca Raton patients get that same hands-on exam and initial care without the drive, which can matter when documentation within Florida's 14-day PIP window is the priority. Office visits at the Deerfield Beach location remain available for anyone who prefers them or whose case isn't eligible for a home visit.

How claim denials are challenged

Florida law limits how an insurer can stop paying for chiropractic care already underway: under Florida Statute 627.736, an insurer generally cannot withdraw payment for a treating provider without a valid report from a provider licensed under that same chapter. Since chiropractors are licensed under Chapter 460, a report from a medical doctor alone is not enough on its own to justify cutting off chiropractic care. Reimbursement for eligible services is generally 80% of the reasonable, medically necessary expense. This is general information about how PIP claims work, not legal advice — a licensed attorney can advise on your specific claim.
